"The most important requirement for the development of ecotourism is the sustainable use and conservation of nature."

At Manu Adventures, we believe that nature is not only visited, it is lived. For more than 33 years, we have guided travelers to discover the extraordinary biodiversity of Manu National Park, offering authentic experiences in one of the most biodiverse regions in the world, one of the planet’s natural treasures.

Our journeys combine adventure, safety, and deep respect for nature, inviting every visitor to connect with the jungle and become part of the commitment to protect it for future generations.

Manu National Park

Manu National Park is one of the largest and most biodiverse natural areas in South America. Located between the mountains of Cusco and the rainforest of Madre de Dios, it protects nearly two million hectares stretching from the Andes to the Amazon, creating an extraordinary diversity of ecosystems.

Recognized by UNESCO as a World Biosphere Reserve, Manu is home to one of the greatest concentrations of life on the planet. Here live iconic species such as the jaguar, spectacled bear, giant river otter, and black caiman, as well as thirteen species of primates and more than one thousand species of birds, including colorful macaws.

Its forests contain nearly 10% of the world’s plant species, with extraordinary richness: up to 220 species of trees can grow in a single hectare. Manu is undoubtedly one of Earth’s great natural treasures, a sanctuary of life that reminds us of the importance of protecting and conserving our planet.

Day 1

Cusco (3,300 m.a.s.l.) – Andes (3,650 m.a.s.l.) – Cloud Forest (1,400 m.a.s.l.)

Departure from Cusco very early in the morning in one of our vehicles. We will cross interesting inter-Andean valleys and picturesque farming communities. Our adventure begins with a visit to the Chullpas of Ninamarca, pre-Inca funeral towers from the Lupaca culture, an Aymara cultural group that developed along the shores of Lake Titicaca. Later, we will visit Paucartambo, a picturesque colonial town and its interesting museum.

Our next stop is the pass of Acjanacco, the first control post and ranger station. Crossing this natural border, we enter the Amazon Basin, where a gradual descent begins into the Cloud Forest and its diverse ecosystems.

This area is rich in endemic species such as the spectacled bear and the Andean cock-of-the-rock, along with orchids, bromeliads, ferns, lichens, and more. We will enjoy nature walks before continuing to our lodge Orquídeas de San Pedro, where we will spend our first comfortable night.

Day 2

Cloud Forest – Erika Lodge (600 m.a.s.l.)

Very early in the morning, we will search for Peru’s national bird, the Rupicola peruvianus, also known as the Andean cock-of-the-rock or Tunqui, famous for its unique courtship ritual.

After breakfast, we continue descending through dense and lush forests until reaching the town of Pilcopata*, and later the port of Atalaya, located on the banks of the Alto Madre de Dios River at 600 m.a.s.l.

From there, we begin a motorboat journey to observe the enormous diversity of bird species such as herons, vultures, cormorants, and more until arriving at our Private Reserve “Erika”.

After lunch, we will visit Machuwasi Lake, where we quietly paddle on a catamaran to observe a wide variety of birds including the prehistoric Hoatzin, horned screamers, red-headed cardinals, capped herons, vultures, and cormorants. During our walk, we may also observe monkeys, capybaras, snakes, and other species.

Later, we will walk along jungle trails searching for diverse flora and fauna, and enjoy a night walk to observe insects and amphibians active during this time.

*Optional Rafting: From Pilcopata, we will enjoy one and a half hours of rafting (Class I and II) along the Koñeq and/or Alto Madre de Dios rivers. We will enjoy the scenery and swim in the refreshing waters.

Day 3

Erika Lodge - Cusco

Very early in the morning, we board our boat and head toward the clay lick along the Alto Madre de Dios River, where different species of parrots such as blue-headed parrots, white-eyed parakeets, and possibly the Maracana macaw gather each morning to consume clay necessary for their diet.

After this daily spectacle, we return to Erika Lodge to pack and continue by boat to Atalaya Port, where our vehicle will be waiting to take us back.

Along the route, we will enjoy one final adventure: a visit to the hummingbird garden Mirador Pico de Hoz (750 m.a.s.l.), a self-sustaining environmental conservation center where we can observe and photograph a great variety of hummingbirds and other wildlife such as toucans, king vultures, monkeys, and with luck, a sloth.

Lunch will be served along the route before arriving in Cusco in the afternoon. END OF OUR SERVICES.
