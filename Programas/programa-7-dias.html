"The most important requirement for the development of ecotourism is the sustainable use and conservation of nature."

At Manu Adventures we believe that nature is not only visited, it is lived. For over 33 years we have been taking travelers to discover the extraordinary biodiversity of Manu National Park, offering authentic experiences in one of the most biodiverse regions on the planet, one of the world's natural jewels.

Our trips combine adventure, safety, and a deep respect for nature, inviting each visitor to connect with the jungle and become part of the commitment to protect it for future generations.

Manu National Park

Manu National Park is one of the most extensive and biodiverse natural areas in South America. Located between the mountains of Cusco and the jungle of Madre de Dios, it protects nearly two million hectares ranging from the Andes to the Amazon, creating an extraordinary diversity of ecosystems.

Recognized by UNESCO as a World Biosphere Reserve, Manu is home to one of the highest concentrations of life on the planet. Here live emblematic species such as the jaguar, the spectacled bear, the giant river otter, and the black caiman, as well as thirteen species of primates and over a thousand species of birds, including colorful macaws.

Its forests contain nearly 10% of the world's plant species, with extraordinary richness: in a single hectare, up to 220 species of trees can grow. Manu is undoubtedly one of the great natural treasures of the Earth, a sanctuary of life that reminds us of the importance of protecting and conserving our planet.

Day 1

Cusco (10,800 ft.) – Andes- (12,000 ft.) Cloud Forest (4,600 ft.)

Departure from the city of Cusco very early in the morning in one of our vehicles. We will cross interesting inter-Andean valleys and picturesque farming communities. Our adventure begins by visiting the Chullpas of Ninamarca, pre-Inca funerary towers of the Lupaca culture, an Aymara cultural group that developed on the shores of Lake Titicaca. Then we will visit Paucartambo, a picturesque colonial town and its interesting museum. Our next stop is at the pass of Acjanacco, the first control post and ranger station. Upon crossing this natural border, we enter the "Amazon Basin". From there begins a gradual descent towards the Cloud Forest and different ecosystems. This area is very rich in endemic species such as the Spectacled Bear and the Andean Cock-of-the-rock, orchids, bromeliads, ferns, lichens, etc. We will take walks observing nature and then continue to our lodge Orquídeas de San Pedro where we will spend our first night in comfortable accommodations.

Day 2

San Pedro Cloud Forest – Erika Lodge (2,000 ft.)

Very early we will search for the national bird of Peru, the Rupicola Peruvianus / Andean Cock-of-the-rock or Tunqui, which performs a unique courtship ritual. After breakfast, we continue our descent through these dense and lush forests until we reach the town of Pilcopata*, later to the port of Atalaya, a place located on the banks of the Alto Madre de Dios River, at 2,000 ft. elevation. From here we will begin our motorboat descent towards the Hummingbird Lodge, where we will settle in. Later we can take some walks on the interesting trails found around. Overnight at the Lodge.

*Optional Rafting: From Pilcopata we will do 1.5 hours of rafting (classes I and II) on the Koñeq and/or Alto Madre de Dios rivers. You will enjoy the landscape and swim in its refreshing waters.

Day 3

Hummingbird Lodge – Restricted Zone / Salvador Lake (970 ft.)

After a delicious breakfast we continue descending the Alto Madre de Dios River. We head towards the heart of the Park, one of the most pristine and protected areas on the planet, the Restricted Zone. This biosphere reserve is located on both sides of the Manu River and is dedicated exclusively to tourism and research (400,000 hectares), protected from human impact, interacting since the creation of our planet.

During this part of our tour we will see birds such as skimmers, herons, several species of kingfishers, swallows, and flycatchers.

We will make a brief stop at the Limonal Ranger Station where we will sign the visitor logbook that controls entry to the Reserve. We will continue along the Manu River towards the heart of the reserve for approximately 4 hours until Salvador Lake, leaving all traces of civilization behind. We will spend the next two nights in our camp. During this trip it is common to observe a great variety of bird species such as macaws, herons, roseate spoonbills, also capybaras, both types of caimans, river turtles, monkeys, and with luck some felines that often sunbathe on the beaches or feed on the surrounding beaches. In the afternoon/evening we will dedicate our time to walking in search of wildlife in this pristine forest.

Day 4

Restricted Zone / Salvador Lake (970 ft.)

Most likely we will be woken up early by the howler monkey declaring its territory. Before breakfast we will take a morning walk since the temperature at this time is more pleasant and makes it easier to search for animals that are looking for food. Later we will explore Salvador Lake by catamaran and try to see the giant otter, an endangered species. They can be seen fishing, playing, or swimming. We will also look for the Black Caiman, the Hoatzin, and the surrounding interesting flora. There will be an opportunity to visit a native community, Matshiguenka House, thus interacting with their totally traditional life of hunters, gatherers, fishermen, and farmers.

We will visit Otorongo Lake and the 15-meter-high observation tower, which will allow us to fully appreciate the landscape of the surrounding primary forest and it is more likely that we will see the otter families that reside here. We will have the possibility of taking a short night walk to try to find American Bullfrogs, Horned Frogs, Tree Frogs, and a large number of colorful insects.

Day 5

Restricted Zone / Salvador Lake (970 ft.) – Dorado Lodge (1,850 ft.)

We will get up early again to take a walk around our camp and Salvador Lake. You will be amazed by the giant Ceiba trees, colorful butterflies, and curious squirrel, capuchin, and woolly monkeys. After breakfast we will pack, board our boat, and sail downstream along the Manu River. We will keep our eyes open to see peccaries (native pigs), caimans, turtles, and birds. In the afternoon we will arrive at Dorado Lodge, settle in, relax, and have the possibility of taking a short night walk. Overnight at the Lodge.

Day 6

Dorado Lodge – Shintuya Hot Springs – Erika Lodge

After a delicious breakfast we will leave Dorado Lodge and sail along the Alto Madre de Dios River towards the Shintuya Hot Springs, where we will enjoy relaxing natural hot springs. Then we will head to our Erika Private Reserve, where we will walk along the interesting trails found in the transition zone between high and low jungle. Your guide will point out and explain the interesting diversity of wildlife around us.

In the afternoon we will visit Lake Machuwasi, where we will paddle quietly in a catamaran to observe a huge diversity of bird species such as the Hoatzin (prehistoric bird), horned screamers, red-capped cardinals, herons, vultures, cormorants, and others. While walking we can observe monkeys, capybaras, snakes, and other species.

If time permits, we will take a walk on another trail where we will visit the Ceiba tree, one of the largest and oldest Amazonian plant species on the planet. Additionally, we will have the opportunity to take an optional night walk to observe insects and amphibians. We will spend the night at our Erika Lodge.

Day 7

Erika Lodge – Cusco

Very early we will board our boat and head to the Clay Lick, a clay wall next to the Alto Madre de Dios River, where a variety of parrots, such as the blue-headed parrot, white-eyed parakeet, possibly the Maracana macaw, and others come every morning to supplement their diet with minerals and salts.

After breakfast, we continue along the Alto Madre de Dios River, upstream, contemplating the beautiful landscapes of the Amazon jungle and the great diversity of bird species. Before noon we will arrive at the Port of Atalaya, where our vehicle will be waiting to take us back. On the way back to Cusco we will make a brief stop at the Pico de Hoz Viewpoint (2,460 ft.), a self-sustainable environmental conservation center, to observe and photograph a wide variety of hummingbirds and other bird species such as toucans, king vultures, and maybe some monkeys, and with luck, a sloth. Then we will continue our way back, arriving in the city of Cusco in the afternoon. END OF OUR SERVICES.
