"The most important requirement for the development of ecotourism is the sustainable use and conservation of nature."

At Manu Adventures, we believe that nature is not only visited, it is experienced. For more than 33 years, we have taken travelers to discover the extraordinary biodiversity of Manu National Park, offering authentic experiences in one of the most biodiverse regions in the world, one of the planet’s natural treasures.

Our journeys combine adventure, safety, and a deep respect for nature, inviting every visitor to connect with the rainforest and become part of the commitment to protect it for future generations.

Manu National Park

Manu National Park is one of the largest and most biodiverse natural areas in South America. Located between the mountains of Cusco and the rainforest of Madre de Dios, it protects nearly two million hectares stretching from the Andes to the Amazon, creating an extraordinary diversity of ecosystems.

Recognized by UNESCO as a World Biosphere Reserve, Manu is home to one of the greatest concentrations of life on Earth. Iconic species such as the jaguar, spectacled bear, giant river otter, and black caiman live here, along with thirteen species of primates and more than one thousand species of birds, including the colorful macaws.

Its forests contain nearly 10% of the world’s plant species, with extraordinary richness: a single hectare can host up to 220 species of trees. Manu is undoubtedly one of the great natural treasures of the Earth, a sanctuary of life that reminds us of the importance of protecting and preserving our planet.

Day 1

Cusco (3,300 m.a.s.l.) – Andes (3,650 m.a.s.l.) – Cloud Forest (1,400 m.a.s.l.)

Departure from Cusco early in the morning in one of our vehicles. We will cross interesting inter-Andean valleys and picturesque farming communities. Our adventure begins with a visit to the Chullpas of Ninamarca, pre-Inca funerary towers of the Lupaca culture, an Aymara cultural group that developed on the shores of Lake Titicaca. Then we visit Paucartambo, a picturesque colonial town and its interesting local museum.

Our next stop is at Acjanacco, the first control post and ranger station. Crossing this natural boundary, we enter the "Amazon Basin" where a gradual descent begins into the Cloud Forest and different ecosystems. This area is rich in endemic species such as the Spectacled Bear and the Cock-of-the-rock, orchids, bromeliads, ferns, lichens, and more.

We will enjoy nature walks before continuing to our lodge Orquídeas de San Pedro, where we will spend our first night comfortably settled.

Day 2

Cloud Forest – Erika Lodge (600 m.a.s.l.)

Very early in the morning, we will search for Peru’s national bird, the Rupicola Peruvianus / Cock-of-the-rock or Tunqui, known for its unique courtship ritual. After breakfast, we continue descending through these dense and lush forests until we reach the town of Pilcopata*, and later the port of Atalaya, located on the banks of the Alto Madre de Dios River at 600 m.a.s.l.

From there, we begin our motorboat journey to observe the incredible diversity of bird species such as herons, vultures, cormorants, and more, until arriving at our Private Reserve "Erika".

After lunch, we visit Machuwasi Lake, where we will peacefully paddle on a catamaran to observe a huge variety of bird species such as the Hoatzin, a prehistoric bird, horned screamers, red-headed cardinals, capped herons, vultures, cormorants, and more. During the walk, we may also observe monkeys, capybaras, snakes, and other species.

Later, we will walk along trails in search of diverse flora and fauna, and we will also have the opportunity for a night walk to observe insects and amphibians native to this area and schedule.

*Optional Canoeing: From Pilcopata, we will enjoy one and a half hours of canoeing (Class I and II rapids) on the Koñeq and/or Alto Madre de Dios rivers. We will enjoy the scenery and swim in the refreshing waters.

Day 3

Erika Lodge

Very early in the morning, we board our boat and head to the parrot clay lick, a clay wall beside the Alto Madre de Dios River, where different species of parrots such as the blue-headed parrot, white-eyed parakeet, possibly the Maracaná Macaw, and others come every morning to supplement their diet with minerals and salts.

After this activity, we return to Erika Lodge for breakfast. Later, we visit Machuwasi Lake, where we paddle peacefully on a catamaran to observe a huge diversity of bird species such as the hoatzin, horned screamers, red-headed cardinals, capped herons, vultures, cormorants, and others. During the walk, we may observe monkeys, capybaras, snakes, and other species.

In the afternoon, we will take another trail walk where we will visit the Ceiba tree, one of the largest and oldest trees on the planet.

Day 4

Erika Lodge - Cusco

After a delicious breakfast in the morning, we pack our belongings and take our boat back to the port of Atalaya, where our vehicle will be waiting to take us back to Cusco.

On the return journey, we will have our final adventure: a visit to the hummingbird garden Mirador Pico de Hoz (750 m.a.s.l.), a self-sustaining environmental conservation center where we can observe and photograph a wide variety of hummingbirds and other birds such as toucans, king vultures, monkeys, and with luck, a sloth.

We will have lunch on the way back to Cusco and arrive in the city in the afternoon. END OF OUR SERVICES.
